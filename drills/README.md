# The rehearsal

Chapter 28. One timed rehearsal with another person does more than the rest of
Part IV, because the failure modes are interpersonal and none of them is
observable from the inside.

## The protocol

1. **Find a person.** They do not need to be an engineer. They need to answer
   in character and hold back what the brief tells them to hold back.
2. **Give them a role brief from `role-briefs/`** — one in a domain you know
   nothing about. Send it to them; do not read it yourself.
3. **Full length, timed, screen shared, recorded.** Not a shortened version:
   the failure modes appear in the second half.
4. **Do not stop when something breaks.** That is the most valuable part of the
   recording.
5. **Do not watch it back for thirty minutes.** You will not see it usefully
   before then.
6. **Score it from the recording** with `scoring.md`, not from memory.

## For whoever is playing the owner

Thank you. Your job is harder than it looks and the whole exercise depends on
you doing two things.

**Answer what you are asked, not what you think they need.** If they ask a
vague question, give a vague answer. If they ask "how many orders a day?",
"a few?" is a perfect answer. You are a real person with a real business, and
you have no idea which details matter.

**Hold back what the brief says to hold back.** There is a section headed *what
you do not say until asked outright*. It always contains a second person who
uses the system and a constraint that changes the design. If they never ask,
they never find out, and they build the wrong thing — **that is the point of
the exercise and it is not unfair.** It is what a real session would have done
to them.

**Deliver the four interjections at the times given.** Not early, not late. One
of them lands after they have started building, and it is the most informative
minute of the whole rehearsal.

Otherwise: be interested, be a bit impatient, be uninterested in technology,
and be specific about what annoys you.

## Rehearsing the other formats

**Take-home.** Three-hour timer, unfamiliar brief, keep to it. Score two
things only: was there a failing check in the first half-hour, and did you stop
on time.

**AI-free.** Build something small without assistance, timed. If you have been
working with assistance daily this is slower than you expect, and the gap is
not predictable from the inside.
