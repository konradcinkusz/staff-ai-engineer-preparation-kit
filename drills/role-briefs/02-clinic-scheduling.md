# Role brief — Dev, who runs a physiotherapy clinic

**For the person playing Dev. Do not let the candidate read this.**

Four physiotherapists, one room each, one receptionist. You are one of the
four. Precise, slightly impatient, and you have opinions about software because
the last system was terrible.

## What you know, and will say if asked

- Appointments are 30 or 45 minutes. A first appointment is always 45.
- Patients book by ringing reception, or by walking in.
- Each physiotherapist has their own patients mostly, but not strictly — a
  patient can see anybody if their usual one is away.
- The receptionist keeps a paper day-sheet per physiotherapist.
- What you need to record: who, which physio, when, how long, first visit or
  not, and a note.
- Cancellations happen constantly. Maybe a fifth. People just do not come.
- "How many a day?" — "Each of us sees eight or nine. So thirty-something."

## How you behave

- More precise than most owners, so give real numbers when asked directly.
- Impatient with abstraction. If they talk about entities, say "sorry, what
  does that mean for me?"
- Specific about the last system: it let reception book two people into one
  slot and nobody noticed until they were both in the waiting room.

## What you do NOT say until asked outright

- **There is a second kind of user.** Patients have started asking to book
  online. You have not decided whether you want that, and it changes
  everything — a patient must not be able to see anybody else's appointments,
  and you have never had to think about that before. Only say this if they ask
  who else touches this, or about the future, or about who is allowed to see
  what.
- **Physiotherapists have holidays**, and when one is away their patients get
  moved to whoever is free. You do this by hand and it takes an evening. Only
  if they ask what happens when somebody is away.
- **Room availability matters.** Four physios, three rooms — one of you works
  from a shared room on Tuesdays. Only if they ask what constrains a booking
  beyond the person's time.

## The four interjections

**~12 minutes.** "Does it matter that an initial assessment is 45 minutes and
the rest are 30? I feel like that's going to matter."

**~40 minutes.** "What happens if reception is on the phone and I'm in the
room, and we both try to put someone in at four o'clock?"

**~65 minutes — *after they have started building*.** "I've just remembered.
Insurance ones have to have a referral number on them, and if it isn't there we
can't bill. Can that be a thing you have to fill in?"

**~85 minutes.** "Be honest with me — how much of what's on the screen do you
actually understand?"
