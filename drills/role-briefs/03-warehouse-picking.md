# Role brief — Sofia, warehouse supervisor

**For the person playing Sofia. Do not let the candidate read this.**

A small warehouse for an online shop selling garden tools. Six pickers. You
have been doing this nine years and you know exactly what is wrong; you do not
know what to do about it.

## What you know, and will say if asked

- Orders come in from the website. Somebody prints them. Pickers walk the aisles
  with a printed sheet and a trolley.
- A picker picks, brings it to packing, packing boxes it and prints a label.
- What is on a sheet: order number, items, quantities, aisle and shelf.
- "How many orders?" — "Two hundred a day. Three hundred before Christmas."
- What goes wrong: an item is not where the sheet says, or it is not there at
  all, and then the picker walks back and asks, and the order sits.
- You know the stock numbers are wrong. Everybody knows. Nobody knows by how
  much.

## How you behave

- Blunt and concrete. You talk about aisles and trolleys, not about systems.
- Slightly sceptical that software will help, because the last three things
  head office bought did not.
- Very specific about the walking. Distance is the thing you actually care
  about and you will keep bringing it up.

## What you do NOT say until asked outright

- **There is a second actor.** Goods-in. A delivery arrives, somebody books it
  in, and until they do the stock number is wrong — so a picker can be sent for
  something that is physically on a pallet by the door. Only say this if they
  ask where stock numbers come from, or who else changes them.
- **Two pickers can be sent for the last one of something.** It happens a
  couple of times a week. Whoever gets there first wins and the other order
  goes into a pile that somebody deals with at five o'clock. Only if they ask
  what happens when something runs out, or about two people at once.
- **You want the sheets ordered by aisle, not by order.** You have wanted this
  for years. Nobody asked. Only say it if they ask how the picker decides where
  to go, or what would save time.

## The four interjections

**~12 minutes.** "Does it matter that some things are in two places? Big items
are in the yard, not in the aisles."

**~40 minutes.** "What happens when the picker gets there and it isn't there?
Because that's every day, that one."

**~65 minutes — *after they have started building*.** "Head office want to know
how long an order takes from coming in to going out. Can you do that? They've
been asking for a year."

**~85 minutes.** "How do I know this won't do what the last one did and tell me
there's fourteen when there's none?"
