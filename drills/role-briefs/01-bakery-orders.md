# Role brief — Anna, who runs a bakery

**For the person playing Anna. Do not let the candidate read this.**

You take cake orders. You are busy, practical, and not interested in
technology. You have run this shop for eleven years.

## What you know, and will say if asked

- Custom cakes for birthdays, weddings, christenings. Also trays of pastries
  for offices, which is a different thing and you will not mention it unless
  asked what else you sell.
- Orders come by telephone, mostly, and people coming in. A few by social
  media, which you hate.
- You write them in a paper diary. The diary is the problem: it is one book,
  you cannot look at it when you are at home, and last month two cakes were
  promised for the same Saturday morning.
- A cake needs: what it is, what it says on it, when it is collected, a
  telephone number, and whether they have paid a deposit.
- Deposits are half. People sometimes do not pay one and you let them because
  you know them.
- Busy weeks are before Christmas and through May and June. "How many?"
  — "I don't know. More. A lot more."

## How you behave

- Vague about numbers. If asked how many orders a day, say "a few?" and if
  pressed, "maybe five, more on a Friday."
- Specific about what annoys you: the double-booked Saturday, and people who
  ring to ask whether their order is in.
- Uninterested in how it works. If they explain a database, say "as long as I
  can see it on my phone."
- You answer what you are asked. You do not volunteer.

## What you do NOT say until asked outright

- **There is a second person.** Your daughter Marta does the weekends and needs
  to see the orders too. She has her own phone, she is not always in the shop,
  and she has twice made a cake that was already made. Only say this if they
  ask who else works there, who else needs to see this, or what happens when
  you are not in.
- **A cake can be cancelled**, and then you keep the deposit, and you have no
  idea how many times that happened last year because it is not in the diary.
  Only say this if they ask what happens when something goes wrong.
- **You want to stop taking orders for a date once it is full.** You have never
  said this out loud. Only say it if they ask what you would want it to stop
  you doing.

## The four interjections

Deliver at these elapsed times, whatever they are doing.

**~12 minutes.** "Oh — I should have said. Some of the office ones are every
week, the same order. Does that change anything?"

**~40 minutes.** "Can I just see it on my phone? I don't want to log in to
anything."

**~65 minutes — *after they have started building*.** "One thing. If Marta and
I are both looking at the Saturday at the same time, and we both take an order
— that's the thing that actually happened. Does yours stop that?"

**~85 minutes.** "How do you know this works? You've had the computer write
most of it."
