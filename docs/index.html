Staff AI Engineer Preparation Kit

A handbook you read once, artefacts you fill in per company, and five cards you print before a call — for an experienced engineer who is not an AI Engineer by title and is aiming at Staff level.

The handbook (PDF) Source, templates and drills

Built from source on every push. The templates and the rehearsal drills are in the repository rather than in the PDF, because you fill them in.

What is in it

  • Part I · The map What the job is, what transfers from the work you already do, and what the word Staff is doing in the ladder.
  • Part II · The evidence What you build before applying, when your CV does not say the title, and the limit of what an artefact can substitute for.
  • Part III · The process The loop, what each stage measures, and the go/no-go you run before you apply rather than after four rounds.
  • Part IV · The build rounds Three formats — AI-assisted, take-home, and the AI-free round that has not gone away — and the craft each one needs.
  • Part V · The bank Questions ordered by how often each surface is reported to be asked, so the order is itself the advice about what to study first.

The cards

One A4 side each, checked by the build. Meant to be on the desk, not on a screen.

  • Run sheet — checkpoints with times, and what survives each block
  • Decision method — the four questions, for answering anything
  • Failure tree — when it breaks, with a time budget per branch
  • Pre-call briefing — what you read in the ten minutes before
  • Banned claims — what your artefacts cannot support, and the recovery line

What it refuses to do

No figures it cannot source. No salary bands, no premiums, no pass rates. Those are regional, stale within two quarters, and mostly unsourced where they circulate. What is here instead is the method for measuring your own market.

No company, and no case study. A build gate refuses any named employer, any individual and any currency figure, so the kit cannot drift back towards the one process it came out of. The method is general or it is not worth having.

No claims it could not trace. Almost nothing in this subject can be measured, so the kind of every claim is marked on the page: a superscript number means it is reported and the source is in the register, with the date it was checked and how good it is. A claim resting on one source says so in the sentence. A diamond means the author's judgement, and you are invited to disagree. Every chapter ends with what this chapter does not claim, and an appendix names the figures every competing resource repeats and says why each one is not here.
